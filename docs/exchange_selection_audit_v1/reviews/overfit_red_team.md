## Preregistration and evidence-stage findings

The fixed rule in `SCORING_PREREGISTRATION.json` is byte-identical to commit `055bcc27c7dd92cf3934b0adfde519d8b3f53092` (`ab7add22e1ab5efd543499729c431e03fda1fd6f213063c94bbbf6614e9ce699` SHA-256). The commit is dated 2026-10-12 20:19:12 UTC, before the earliest request timestamp located in the twelve source indexes (Hyperliquid, 20:20:42.267830 UTC). Both weight vectors sum to 100. Unknown binary indicators score zero, missing fee or book makes the whole corresponding cost criterion zero, and missing hard prerequisites override numerical rank. The sensitivity recipe is fixed: cost weight 30% or 40% with all other execution weights scaled proportionally; remove each book snapshot in turn, setting book-based scores to zero when fewer than two remain; treat documentary-only old depth conservatively; keep promotions excluded.

For the two venue directories added after the first independent data review, all indexed raw hashes that declared both a path and SHA-256 verified locally: Coinbase International 43/43 and Deribit 56/56. This verifies retained bytes, not economic or product interpretation.

The evidence does **not** show a complete 2–3-year M15 bar sequence, an audited missing-bar policy, or recovered historic tick/filter epochs for any venue. Actual 2024-10-13 00:00 UTC native BTC and ETH M15 rows establish only bounded point reachability for OKX, Bitget, BloFin, Kraken Futures, Deribit and Coinbase International. The latter has both current products PAUSED and empty current-window M15. The hard research prerequisite is the actual old sentinel or official archive, current defensible filters, product identity and causal prospective capture. Historic filter epochs are an explicit later backtest limitation but are **not** an extra hard research prerequisite in the frozen rule. A rank must not imply historical executable backtest readiness.

The primary all-in cost can use only a credible ordinary/API trading-class taker fee and exact-unit, valid BTC/ETH books. Public contract fee fields for OKX, Gate and MEXC do not resolve ordinary API-account applicability; Binance Futures and Coinbase International also lack a verified numeric ordinary schedule. These must get zero cost and fail the fee-related execution hard gate if unresolved. Public ordinary schedules for Deribit, Hyperliquid, Kraken Futures, Bybit and BloFin have stronger applicability evidence; Bitget and KuCoin Futures public instrument rates carry an actual-account caveat. VIP, token, referral, app and selected-pair promotions are excluded. Funding is separately signed, not added to commission based on a single print.

Book quantity units are directly supported for OKX, BloFin, Gate, Hyperliquid, KuCoin Futures and MEXC. Bitget depth calls the second field “Quantity” without proving denomination, while the separate order schema uses base coin; Kraken PF book “size” has no direct denomination proof in retained official docs. A base-quantity multiplier of one for either is an inference. Book-derived liquidity and cost for those two should remain zero until the unit is proved, with the assumption and its numerical effect exposed only as a separate scenario. Kraken raw bids are ascending; a walker must sort bids descending and asks ascending. All five notionals on both sides need full fill, with no extrapolation.

The first draft of `reviews/SCORING_ASSESSMENTS.json` marked Bitget and Kraken research `cost_realism` indicator 4 (“two snapshots and all five USD sizes both sides covered”) true while setting `book_units_confirmed=false`. The unit gap prevents proving those USD sizes are fully fillable. I challenged this before final scoring; the statistician agreed and changed both indicators to zero. Its broad repeated source-ID bundles still need relevance checking for each affirmative indicator.

The original cloud books contain two or three observations per symbol, separated by minutes to about ten hours; CI later supplied additional retained valid books. Venues were not sampled at identical UTC instants. Median and worst observed samples are bounded diagnostics, not an execution guarantee. For any venue with exactly two valid samples, every leave-one-out run would have fewer than two and force book-based liquidity and cost to zero under the frozen sensitivity rule. A resulting role flip is evidence of ranking fragility, not permission to pick a favorable sample or change the rule.

Execution hard gates need particular care. Hyperliquid's agent-wallet transfer/withdrawal scope is unproved by the retained excerpts; its scoped-signer equivalence is unresolved. Deribit documents a dedicated subaccount alternative to position-level isolated margin, but equivalence to the required protective control has not been established. KuCoin Futures' trade-without-withdraw scope is documented by separate endpoint permissions, while key-level configuration and IP binding remain unverified; the frozen hard gate turns on withdrawal separation, not optional IP binding alone. Bybit cloud 403 and Binance Futures cloud 451 are real access evidence; a CI success would show a second route's accessibility but would not erase cloud blocking. Account mode, jurisdiction, and actual fee tier are later activation checks for a selected architecture and cannot be asserted for this user from a public-only audit.

## Final scoring and sensitivity review

The root's completed score artifacts and ordinary GitHub-hosted CI receipts were reviewed. CI public instrument/book probes succeeded at OKX, Bitget, BloFin, Gate, Hyperliquid, Kraken Futures, KuCoin Futures, MEXC and Deribit. Bybit remained HTTP 403, Binance Futures HTTP 451, and Coinbase International current BTC/ETH book routes HTTP 404 with paused products. The final VWAP sample uses all retained valid local and CI books under the frozen “all retained valid captures” rule, with source identity and exact unit checks; it does not pick the most favorable source. Gate zero-quantity price levels contribute no executable depth. The supplemental current lot-grid calculation is explicitly separate from the frozen ranking and still leaves queue, future fill and historical metadata questions for the compatibility gate.

I independently recomputed every research and execution weighted score from the published five-indicator counts and fixed weights using rational arithmetic: 12/12 research and 12/12 execution rows match. Each published eligibility flag matches the five frozen hard prerequisites, including exclusion of Deribit and Hyperliquid from execution despite their numerically higher scores. I also recomputed all 24 scores for the ±5-percentage-point execution-cost shifts using exact proportional redistribution; all match. Unknown or false indicators remain zero, unknown book units give Bitget and Kraken zero book-derived cost/liquidity, and unknown base API fees give OKX, Gate, MEXC, Binance Futures and Coinbase International zero cost and failed execution fee gates. No promotion or funding print enters the primary all-in cost.

Research selection is a **true 82–82 tie** between eligible Bitget and OKX; Bitget is named primary only by the frozen lexicographic slug tie-break, while OKX is the benchmark. This is not evidence that Bitget is economically or statistically superior. BloFin is the highest eligible execution venue at 69; its 12.12725964327464574 bps taker/taker fee-plus-book estimate is a bounded snapshot model. The primary research and primary execution roles remain Bitget and BloFin under both ±5 cost-weight shifts, the conservative unknown-depth scenario, and all 76 leave-one-book runs. This is robustness of the fixed architecture ranking to those specified perturbations, not a guarantee of future liquidity, fees or fills.

The **secondary execution role is fragile**. Kraken Futures and KuCoin Futures tie at 29 after the frozen missing-data penalty, so Kraken is selected lexicographically. One of 76 leave-one-book runs removes a shallow CI KuCoin ETH snapshot (`e7c1a75f8f72a9f59da3aad8.receipt.json`), restores all-size visible fillability for KuCoin, and changes the secondary role to KuCoin. In that retained snapshot the $50,000 ETH BUY is short of visible quote depth by $13,819.9774; this is a single 20-level observation, not proof that KuCoin is generally illiquid. Kraken's book quantity units remain unproved and its slippage is unknown. The secondary role is therefore a watchlist architecture choice, not an operational fallback approval.

I find no red-team blocker to `EXCHANGE_SELECTION_AUDIT_FROZEN_PASS` **as an architecture selection only**, provided the final report states both ties, the secondary sensitivity flip, the fee/book unknowns, and the historical limitations. The audit does not qualify a historical executable backtest, actual account/jurisdiction, prospective OOS clock, paper trading or live trading. Those remain later gates; no strategy rule or scoring parameter is changed here.
